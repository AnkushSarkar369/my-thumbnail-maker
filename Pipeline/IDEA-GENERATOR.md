# IDEA GENERATOR

This file defines the upstream creative layer for thumbnail ideation.

The Thumbnail System and `Pipeline/PROMPT-MAKER.md` execute a thumbnail concept. This file determines what that concept should be when no strong concept has already been supplied.

## Scope Isolation

This file is an ideation tool, not a visual design manual.

When using this file, do **not** read, consult, or apply any of the following as creative or visual constraints:

- `System/READ THIS FIRST.md`
- `System/ANKUSH.md`
- `System/AI-REJECTION.md`
- `Skills/COMPOSITION.md`
- `Skills/TYPOGRAPHY.md`
- `Skills/COLOUR.md`
- `Skills/IMAGE.md`
- `Pipeline/PROMPT-MAKER.md`

Do not use the Thumbnail System to narrow the ideation space. The purpose of this stage is to discover the strongest truthful package first. Visual execution constraints are applied downstream.

## Objective

Maximize CTR while truthfully reflecting the video.

CTR is the primary optimization target. Do not optimize for aesthetic taste, brand consistency, originality for its own sake, educational completeness, or visual sophistication unless those things contribute to getting the click.

The thumbnail does not need to be a literal summary of the video. It needs to create the strongest credible reason to click while remaining defensible once the viewer watches the video.

## Input

Start from the actual video:

- title or working title
- script, transcript, or detailed summary
- central argument
- strongest claims, conflicts, surprises, contradictions, transformations, questions, or stakes
- intended audience when known
- any thumbnail idea already supplied by Ankush

Understand what the video actually delivers before inventing packaging.

## Core Process

### 1. Find the click-worthy core

Identify what is most psychologically compelling about the video.

Look for:

- contradiction
- curiosity gap
- surprising consequence
- tension
- taboo or forbidden information
- threat or loss
- desire or aspiration
- status conflict
- transformation
- mystery
- absurdity
- strong emotional reaction
- recognizable problem
- unexpected mechanism
- identity conflict
- visually demonstrable claim

Do not assume the title's literal subject is the strongest packaging subject.

### 2. Separate the idea from its obvious visual representation

Ask what the video is fundamentally about, then ask how that idea could be represented visually.

Abstract ideas should be given concrete visual form when useful. Do not force an analogy when a literal representation is stronger.

A graph, split-screen, physical metaphor, transformation, constructed scene, juxtaposition, character state, object interaction, photographic situation, or other visual mechanism may be appropriate.

The visual representation may introduce information that never literally appears in the video if it truthfully communicates the video's underlying idea.

### 3. Generate from outside the user's existing vocabulary

Do not restrict ideation to Ankush's own references, previous thumbnails, or self-improvement conventions.

Search conceptually across mechanisms used by:

- advertising
- film and television posters
- magazine covers
- journalism
- documentary photography
- political communication
- product marketing
- sports
- gaming
- memes
- horror
- comedy
- luxury branding
- propaganda and persuasion
- editorial design
- visual art
- information design
- psychology

Borrow the underlying attention mechanism, not another domain's aesthetic by default.

The purpose is to expand the solution space, not to make the thumbnail look like an advertisement, movie poster, meme, etc.

### 4. Challenge the obvious idea

If Ankush supplies a thumbnail concept, treat it as a valuable creative hypothesis, not an instruction that must win.

Preserve it as a candidate, then independently search for stronger representations.

Ask:

- Is there a more clickable framing?
- Can the same idea produce a stronger curiosity gap?
- Is there a contradiction that can be made visible?
- Can two familiar things be juxtaposed in an unfamiliar way?
- Can the viewer understand the setup instantly but still need the video to resolve it?
- Is the concept visually distinctive in a feed full of competing thumbnails?
- Does the image make the viewer ask a specific question?

Do not reject an unusual user idea merely because a more conventional thumbnail would be easier to execute.

### 5. Generate multiple genuinely different mechanisms

Do not produce cosmetic variations of one idea.

When ideation is requested, explore materially different constructions. For example, a topic might be represented through:

- direct depiction
- contradiction
- visual metaphor
- before/after
- split reality
- scale manipulation
- social/status comparison
- object substitution
- absurd juxtaposition
- diagram or system
- environmental storytelling
- facial/emotional transformation
- mystery or partial concealment

The number of ideas matters less than the diversity of mechanisms.

### 6. Evaluate for CTR

Judge concepts primarily by:

1. Immediate readability at thumbnail size.
2. Strength of the curiosity gap.
3. Emotional or psychological pull.
4. Distinctiveness in a recommendation feed.
5. Simplicity of the first read.
6. Ability to create a question the viewer wants answered.
7. Compatibility with the title.
8. Strength without requiring tiny explanatory text.
9. Truthfulness to what the video actually delivers.
10. Feasibility within the Thumbnail System.

A beautiful image with weak curiosity is not a strong CTR concept.

A concept that perfectly summarizes the video but gives the viewer no reason to click is not a strong CTR concept.

A highly clickable concept that materially misrepresents the video is also unacceptable.

## Title–Thumbnail Relationship

Treat title and thumbnail as one packaging unit.

They should not simply repeat the same information.

Prefer combinations where one element establishes the premise and the other creates tension, curiosity, contrast, specificity, or an unanswered question.

If the title already explains the claim, the thumbnail can create the contradiction.

If the thumbnail communicates the situation immediately, the title can introduce the unresolved idea.

The viewer should be able to understand the package quickly while still having a reason to click.

## Important Boundary

The thumbnail is allowed to be rhetorically stronger than the video's literal wording.

It does not need to be politically neutral, academically qualified, or a complete representation of the argument.

It may exaggerate emphasis, use contrast, use metaphor, or deliberately create tension.

The boundary is deception: do not promise a substantially different video, claim an outcome the video does not support, or manufacture a premise that the video cannot satisfy.

## Output

When asked to ideate, return a small set of the strongest materially different packaging concepts rather than a giant list.

For each concept provide:

- Core visual construction
- Psychological hook
- Why it could earn the click
- How it pairs with the working title
- Any major execution risk

Then identify the strongest candidate and explain what makes it stronger than the alternatives.

If Ankush is deliberately taking the role of creative director and wants to present his own thought process first, do not prematurely replace it. Analyze his idea alongside independently generated alternatives.

The goal is not to prove the AI more creative than Ankush. The goal is to find the highest-CTR truthful package.
