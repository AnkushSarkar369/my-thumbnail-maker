# Typography

Typography is a visual component of the thumbnail, not merely a label.

## Core Principle

Choose typography according to the role it needs to play in the concept and composition.

Do not use one font or typographic treatment for every thumbnail simply for consistency.

Typography must serve the concept before it serves an aesthetic category such as "luxury," "editorial," "premium," or "sophisticated."

## Typographic Roles

### Bold Sans

Use for short, immediate, high-impact words or statements. This is often the strongest choice when a single concept word is a major structural element.

### Elegant Serif

Use for longer phrases, sophisticated concepts, or when typography should genuinely feel editorial or substantial.

Do not select a serif merely because the subject matter is intellectual, philosophical, or serious. The typography must fit the actual visual language of the thumbnail.

### Sans + Serif

Use when deliberate contrast between contemporary and sophisticated qualities strengthens the composition.

### Editorial

Use for quieter supporting information when necessary.

### Script / Handwriting

Use rarely and only when the concept genuinely benefits from a personal, emotional, or handmade quality.

### Soft / Rounded

Use only when the concept specifically calls for a softer or more comfortable character.

## Hierarchy

Typography should have a clear hierarchy.

Determine:

1. What word or phrase matters most?
2. What should be read next?
3. What can remain secondary?

Do not give every word equal visual weight.

## Placement

Place text according to the composition rather than following a fixed text position.

Typography may interact with, overlap, frame, sit behind, or contrast against imagery when doing so improves the concept and readability.

Typography can function as a structural layer of the thumbnail rather than as a caption placed on top of an image.

## Readability

Text must remain legible at thumbnail size.

Prioritize:

- sufficient contrast
- appropriate scale
- clear letterforms
- sensible line spacing
- controlled text density

Do not sacrifice readability for decorative typography.

## Treatment

Typography may use changes in:

- weight
- size
- case
- spacing
- alignment
- colour
- opacity
- layering

Use these deliberately.

Avoid excessive outlines, shadows, gradients, effects, or distortion unless they materially contribute to the concept.

## Consistency

Maintain a small set of approved typefaces for recurring typographic roles.

Consistency should come from repeatedly using the same approved roles and typefaces when appropriate, not from forcing the same typographic treatment onto every thumbnail.

## Final Rule

Typography should strengthen the visual idea and its hierarchy.

Do not choose typography because it makes the thumbnail look more expensive, elegant, cinematic, or sophisticated unless those qualities are actually part of the concept.

If the thumbnail works better without text, do not add text merely because thumbnails are expected to contain it.
