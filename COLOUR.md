# Colour

Colour should support the concept rather than function as a permanent brand palette.

## Core Principle

Default toward controlled, restrained colour.

Neutral, muted, dark, warm, cool, or saturated treatments are all valid when they materially strengthen the idea.

Do not force a recurring accent colour simply to make thumbnails look "branded."

## Palette Selection

Choose the palette based on:

- the video's concept
- the emotional tone
- the subject
- the environment
- the intended visual hierarchy

A thumbnail may use natural colours when the image itself provides enough visual interest.

A deliberately restricted palette may be preferable when it improves clarity or emphasis.

## Saturation

Use saturation deliberately.

Strong colour is appropriate when it creates meaningful emphasis, contrast, or emotional impact.

Muted colour is appropriate when restraint, sophistication, realism, or atmosphere is more important.

Do not increase saturation simply to make the thumbnail more eye-catching.

## Contrast

Use colour contrast to establish hierarchy and readability.

Important elements should separate clearly from their surroundings.

Contrast may come from:

- hue
- brightness
- saturation
- warm/cool relationships
- colour against neutral surroundings

Do not create unnecessary contrast between elements that should visually belong together.

## Skin & Natural Elements

When using photographic imagery, preserve believable skin, materials, and environmental colours.

Do not apply a global colour treatment that makes the image look artificially graded.

## Colour Grading

Colour grading is allowed to change substantially between concepts.

Avoid generic:

- orange-and-teal cinematic grading
- excessive blue/cold grading
- crushed or unnatural colours
- oversaturated HDR-like treatment

The grade should feel intentional and appropriate to the image.

## Final Rule

There is no mandatory Ankush thumbnail colour.

The consistent rule is controlled colour choice: every colour should contribute to the concept, hierarchy, mood, or readability.
