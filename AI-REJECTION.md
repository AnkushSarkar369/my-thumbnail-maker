# AI Rejection

This file defines visual characteristics that should be rejected or corrected when they make an image feel obviously AI-generated or visually artificial.

## Core Principle

Do not judge an image by whether it is technically photorealistic.

Judge whether it has photographic credibility.

An impressive image is still a failure if the viewer can immediately feel that it is synthetic.

## Reject

Reject or correct:

- plastic or unnaturally perfect skin
- synthetic facial features
- excessive skin smoothing
- uniformly smooth surfaces
- impossible or inconsistent anatomy
- incoherent lighting
- shadows that do not agree with the light source
- impossible perspective or scale
- objects occupying physically implausible positions
- repetitive or duplicated elements
- backgrounds filled with unnecessary generated detail
- unnatural crowds or repeated faces
- generic "cinematic" environments
- excessive orange/warm grading
- excessive cold/blue grading
- overprocessed HDR
- excessive sharpening
- excessive blur
- artificial glow or atmospheric effects
- obvious AI compositing

## Backgrounds

Do not maximize the number of objects in a background.

Background elements should occupy believable physical space and have appropriate:

- scale
- depth
- occlusion
- spacing
- focus
- lighting

A complex background is acceptable when it is coherent.

A simple background is preferable when additional detail does not contribute to the concept.

## Lighting

Lighting must make physical sense.

Check:

- direction
- intensity
- softness
- shadows
- reflections
- colour temperature
- interaction with the subject

Do not add dramatic lighting simply because it makes the image appear more impressive.

## Imperfection

Natural imperfections are desirable when appropriate.

Allow realistic:

- skin texture
- small blemishes
- material variation
- lens characteristics
- noise
- imperfect focus
- subtle environmental irregularities

Do not make every surface perfectly clean, sharp, symmetrical, or polished.

## Final Check

Before accepting AI-generated imagery, ask:

"Could this plausibly have been photographed?"

If the answer is no, identify the specific artificial characteristic and correct that characteristic rather than adding more effects.

The objective is not to hide that AI was used.

The objective is to produce imagery with enough physical and photographic credibility that the method of creation is irrelevant.
